# What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Reading the brief's vague abstract requirements and interpreting it was this weeks breakthrough. I asked the agent to recommend several ideas which may satisfy the brief, however, non were to my liking. The README.md requirements on 'good' were also confusingly subjective and previous crits did not prepare me for such a subjective task. Once I figured out what I wanted to do, that's when the brief begun to make a little bit more sense. 

# What it changed about the developer I want to be

I used to treat a brief as a list to deliver, so my first move was to ask the agent what to build, however, the brief was so open-ended, the recommendations didn't sit right. Claude gave me twenty ideas before I had decided what I wanted, which was unrelated to the 20.

I want to be a developer who writes the standard first, even a rough one, and then points the tools at it. Writing "what good means" in the README felt vague, but it gave me something to test the agent's work against. 

It also changed how I think about the weekly crits. Cutting the platform down to login, hosting and a persistent check-in felt like giving something up, considering a full prototype is what I am used to.

Subjective criteria are still hard. Earlier crits had clearer targets, so I never had to defend a standard I wrote myself. What I take from this is that being unsure is not a reason to wait for the agent or the brief to decide for me. I'd rather write a first version, build against it, and revise it when the work shows I was wrong.
